For a month our team read every call summary we wrote, about 200 of them, looking for patterns we had missed while busy.

## The patterns

Roughly 70 of the 200 calls were about the same few topics. Customers often wanted to know when someone would arrive, what the visit would cost, and whether they had to be at home. These questions came up again and again, and the answers were usually the same.

Some calls were longer than they needed to be. When the person answering did not have the booking in front of them, the call took more time. When they did, it was about 4 minutes shorter and the customer seemed happier with the result of the conversation.

Only 12 calls were complaints, and those were the ones people remembered most clearly. They took a lot of energy, though they were a small share of the total.

## What we think it means

We think a good share of calls could be answered by a short message sent the day before a visit, with the arrival window, the price range and whether someone needs to be home. We have not tested this yet, so it is only an idea for now, and the real effect may turn out to be smaller than it looks from reading the summaries.

We also think the people answering the phone need the booking on screen when the call starts. That seems obvious in hindsight, but it was not how the system was set up, and nobody had raised it as an issue during the busy season.

## What we have not worked out

We do not yet know whether customers would read a message the day before, or whether they would call anyway. Some customers prefer to talk to a person, and that is a fair preference that should be respected.

We also do not know how much time this would save in total. Reading the summaries gives a feeling for the problem, but it is not the same as measuring it properly over a period of time with a clear before and after comparison.

## Where this leaves us

Reading the logs was useful. It showed us things that were easy to miss in a busy week, and it gave the team a shared picture of what customers ask about most. The first step is a two-week test of the day-before message.
