Spoke & Sprocket, a fictional nine-person bike repair shop, was losing 19 repair jobs a month because quotes took two days. The owner thought the mechanics were too slow to price jobs. The problem was not the mechanics.

## What was going wrong

The shop got about 60 repair enquiries a month by email and chat. We logged every one for four weeks. The median enquiry waited 7 hours for a first reply, then needed 3 more messages before a mechanic could price it. Most of those messages asked for the same things: the bike model, a photo of the damage and whether the customer needed it back by a date.

Pricing itself took a mechanic about 15 minutes. The two days were spent waiting.

## What we changed

We made two changes and left everything else alone.

1. **A photo checklist in the first reply.** Every new enquiry now gets an instant answer asking for the model, two photos and the deadline. Messages before a quote fell from 3 to 1.
2. **A fixed reply schedule.** The front-desk person answers enquiries at 10am, 2pm and 5pm. Before, nobody owned the inbox, so everyone assumed someone else had replied.

We did not add a quoting tool, a chatbot or new software. The checklist is a saved reply.

## What happened

After eight weeks, the median time to quote was 3 hours instead of 2 days. Lost enquiries fell from 19 a month to 6. The mechanics spent the same 15 minutes pricing each job, but they now got everything they needed in one message instead of four.

Two things did not work. Customers ignored a longer checklist with seven questions, so we cut it to three. And a reply schedule of every hour was too much for one person, so we moved to three fixed times.

## What I would do differently

I would have logged the enquiries before suggesting anything. In the first meeting I proposed a quoting tool, and it would have saved 10 minutes a job while the real wait stayed at two days.

## The lesson for other small shops

If quotes feel slow, count where the time goes before you buy anything. Check one month of enquiries this week: write down the first-reply time, the number of messages before you could price the job, and the pricing time itself. Fix the biggest number first.
